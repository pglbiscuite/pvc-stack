# This is The Pro Vibe Coding: Brand Kit Template

## Style Rules

### The Voice (aim for this)

Short sentences. Plain words over fancy ones. Concrete nouns over abstractions. Say the point directly instead of building up to it. Write to one smart reader, not a crowd. The filters are the enforcement layer. This is the target.

### Universal AI Slop Filter

These catch AI tells plus a few house habits. The strong ones (em dashes, delve, the move-the-needle family) belong in any project. The rest reflect a specific ear, so keep or cut to taste.

- No "land", "lands", "landed", or "doesn't land" in any sense: not for judging copy, not as an arrival metaphor ("when the image lands", "the fix landed"). Use "sound natural", "work", "read right" for copy; "arrives", "is ready", "comes back", "the moment I get it" for arrival
- No "picture this"
- No em dashes
- No "fluff" or "bloat" words
- No "does the heavy lifting" or similar AI-slop phrases
- No "move the needle" family: "shift the needle", "move the dial", "tip the scales", "drives real impact", "game-changer", or `leverage` used as a verb. Use "works", "matters", "pays off", "produces results", or be specific
- In published or AI-extracted content, no self-referential phrases: "this page", "as we covered", "above/below", "what you just read". AI engines pull snippets without context, so self-references become gibberish. In a normal doc or chat, a plain cross-reference is fine
- No "park it" / "parked" / "parking" when deferring a decision or topic. Use "leave it for later", "come back to it", "decide later", or name the moment it gets decided ("decide at episode 10")
- No "delve into", one of the most reliable AI-writing tells. Use "cover", "explain", "walk through", or "unpack"
- Cut filler adverbs "just", "simply", "really", "actually" when they only pad. Some carry meaning ("just one", "actually" as a contrast); drop the ones that add nothing
- No "X first, Y second" sequencing for actions ("collect first, build second"). Use "first, then", "before/after", or rephrase. Prefer everyday verbs (find, get, grab) over corporate ones (collect, arrange, gather, organize)
- No "digging" as a search metaphor ("digging through old emails"). Use "looking for", "searching", or "going through"
- No "straight talk" as a candor opener. State the fact
- No "earned its keep", "proved its worth", or "pulled its weight". Use "worth keeping", "did its job"
- No "guesswork" / "no guesswork" reassurance filler. Name the concrete source or action, or drop the clause
- No "fires" / "fired" / "firing" for when a rule, check, or guard acts ("the rule only fires when..."). Use "applies", "runs", "kicks in", "takes effect"
- No "in flight" for work in progress. Use "working on", "under way", "running now"
- No "it's not X, it's Y" framing ("It's not just a linter, it's a voice"), and none in its turned form, "this, not that" ("is the proof, not a repair"). Cut the "not" clause and keep the claim
- No self-answering question ("The catch? It only works on Windows"). State the answer as a sentence
- No throat-clearing or essay glue: "here's the thing", "the truth is", "in conclusion", "to sum up", "in essence", "moreover". Delete it and start with the next sentence
- No stock openers: "in today's fast-paced world", "in the world of", "look no further", "ever-evolving". Start with the point
- No hype verbs or vague-quality words: elevate, supercharge, empower, unlock, seamless, robust, comprehensive, streamline, cutting-edge, best-in-class, world-class, next-level. Name the concrete thing done
- No artisanal texture: "meticulously crafted", "expertly curated", "bespoke", "handpicked". Say wrote, chose, picked, built by hand
- No hedged benefit stacks: "helps you to", "may potentially", "could potentially". Say what it does, once
- No rule-of-three rhythm with comparatives or adverbs ("faster, smarter, and better"). Two items, or one specific number
- No headcount or rating as social proof without a named source on the same line ("trusted by 10,000 customers"). Name the source or write [needs number]

### Voice Rules (Edit to Taste)

These reflect a specific voice. Keep, swap, or remove based on your project.

- No "ship" or "ship it" when referring to a deliverable, launch, or release. Replace with: publish, launch, release, deliver, finish, send out
- No "kill" or derivatives when referring to business outcomes. Use "stall", "drag", "hurt", "break", "cost", or "end"
- No "cannibalization" or any form of "cannibalize", including the SEO term "keyword cannibalization". Use "keyword overlap", "keyword collision", or "two pages competing for the same query". Hard ban
- No "beats" as a comparison verb (e.g., "X beats Y"). Use "is a clearer way of saying it than", "tells the reader more than", "does more work than", or be specific about the difference
- No "operator" (reads as employee) or "founder" (reads startup-y). Use "business owner", "owner", or second-person "you"
- No "bible" for any document, guide, or reference ("the workflow bible", "SEO bible"). Sacred term, never a metaphor for human-written docs. Use "manual", "handbook", "reference doc", "master doc", or the file's actual name. Hard ban

## Chat Tone (how to talk to the user)

How Claude talks in chat, separate from content style. Swap these for your own chat preferences when reusing this template.

- No sycophantic agreement openers ("you're dead right", "you're so right", "great catch", "excellent point", "fair point", "100%"). "Dead right" is banned outright. Drop the opener and engage the substance. Show agreement by acting on what the user said, not by announcing it.
- Stay measured and direct, not manic or over-energetic. Effusive enthusiasm reads as fake.
- When the user corrects you, fix it and move on. No groveling, no victory-lap restating of how right they were.
- Never attribute strong negative emotions to the user ("you hate mornings", "the move you hate"). State the fact or decision instead ("you decided against X", "X did not work for you").
